The whistle

The one you can't forget

Rule of the bag: the whistle has a home on the outside, at the top, next to the zip pull, in a bright red pocket. An empty red pocket shouts at you. A whistle at the bottom of a bag is a whistle at home.

Which whistle

Buy this week

Pea-less whistle on a lanyard

No pea inside, so nothing jams when it's wet or dusty, and it's loud outdoors. One per trainer, never shared.

Nice upgrade

Electronic whistle

A button, not a mouth. Three tones, loud enough for a field, hygienic between trainers, and it doesn't need breath you'd rather use for talking. Needs batteries, which is why they're on the kit list.

How we use it with kids

ONE BLAST = FREEZE, LOOK AT METWO = COME INNO WHISTLE = KEEP MOVING

Keep it rare. If the whistle goes every 20 seconds it stops meaning anything. Your voice runs the games, the whistle runs the emergencies and the transitions.

“When you hear this [one blast], you freeze like a statue and your eyes find me. Let's practise. Everyone move!”

One blast. Praise the fastest statues. Done, it's learned for the month.

In Traffic Lights the whistle is simply RED. Blast and wand go red together.

The Wand

Concept

A colour baton the trainer holds up. The wand turns green, thirty kids go. Amber, they move in slow motion. Red, they freeze. The game is already in the script; the wand makes it visible from the far side of a field, and it makes the trainer look like they're running the show.

Full-screen colour, tap for the next one. Until the real wand arrives, hold the phone up.

Three builds, pick a lane

Build 1 · Buy it this week

Any LED baton with a push button

The light-up batons sold for parties and concerts. Look for one where each press holds a colour rather than auto-strobing, and set its order to green, amber, red. Costs little, works on day one.

  • Weak point: daylight. Most are built for the dark. Test it outdoors at 8am before you trust it.
  • Keep a spare set of batteries in the Me pouch.
Build 2 · The GTKF Wand

Our own baton, six colours only

A moulded wand in GTKF blue with a frosted tube so the colour reads from 40 metres in full sun. One big thumb button: press for the next colour, hold for random. Nothing else on it.

  • The six game colours and nothing more: green, amber, red, then blue, purple, orange for the add-on round.
  • USB-C rechargeable, one charge lasts a week of sessions, same cable as the phone.
  • Soft rubber tip and a wrist strap. It will be waved near faces.
  • Buzzes in the hand on each change so the trainer can call the colour without looking down.
Build 3 · Synced

Wand and phone show the same colour

The wand pairs to the trainer's phone over Bluetooth. Press the wand and the full-screen lights in the session script change with it; tap the phone and the wand follows. A motion sensor turns a wave into the next colour, so the trainer calls colours with their arm, eyes on the kids.

  • Adds the speaker cue: green plays “go”, red plays a horn.
  • Session log: how many colour calls, how fast. Pure bragging rights for trainers.
  • This is the version that gets a “whoa” from a 7-year-old. It's also the one that needs an engineer, so it's Season 2.

Amp it up: more ideas, same colour system

  1. Colour bands for kids. Silicone wristbands in team colours now. Later: LED wristbands that follow the wand, like a stadium show. Thirty wrists turning green at once.
  2. Lava islands that glow. Flat markers with an LED ring. The wand sets them: green islands are safe, red islands are lava. Floor is Lava, but the floor is alive.
  3. Sound cues from the speaker. Three sounds mapped to the three colours. Kids learn the sound in one session, so it still works when backs are turned.
  4. Attendance on the phone. Tap a name as they arrive, and day-two comebacks are counted for you. Feeds the “who's coming back?” pep talk with real numbers.
Every idea here uses the same six colours and the same three actions the script already teaches. New kit, no new rules for the kids.

The bag

3 concepts

The job of the bag is to make forgetting impossible. Three concepts below, all built on the same six rules. The drawings are layouts, not final looks: GTKF blue and pink, the logo big, so the kids know the bag means the session is on.

The six rules every concept obeys

  1. Whistle pocket on the outside, at the top. Red, next to the zip pull. First in, last out.
  2. Shadow slots. Every item sits on its own printed outline. A missing item is a visible gap, not a memory test.
  3. One zone per job. Signal, Write, Me, Safety. Same four zones as the kit list above.
  4. Opens flat. Packed on a kitchen table, grabbed from on the ground at the edge of the court.
  5. Built for here. Sand-proof zips, light outer colour for the heat, wipe-clean lining, a bottle pocket that takes a full litre.
  6. Looks like the brand. GTKF blue and pink, the logo big. The bag is the first thing the kids see every morning.
Concept A · The Sling

One strap, five things, 20 minutes

A chest sling for the trainer who runs four schools in a morning and walks in with nothing else. The whistle pocket sits on the strap at chest height so the whistle is at your mouth before the bag is even off.

Concept B · The Roll

A fold-out panel that lives in any bag

Not a bag, the brain of one. A canvas roll with a shadow slot for each item. Unroll it on the floor and the whole kit is laid out like a surgeon's tray; anything missing is an empty outline. Roll it, strap it, drop it in whatever backpack the trainer already owns.

Concept C · The Kit Bag

The full GTKF backpack, opens like a suitcase

A clamshell backpack. The front panel unzips all the way round and lies flat, so the bag opens into two halves: the Roll's shadow panel is built into one side, mesh pouches for the softer stuff on the other. Whistle pocket sits at the top handle, the wand runs down the spine in its own sleeve, markers slide into a side sleeve, the bottle gets its own pocket.

Open flat on the floor. Left half: mesh for the soft stuff. Right half: shadow slots for the five non-negotiables plus the cards.
  • Phone pocket on the back panel against the spine, with a cable port into the power-bank pocket, so the phone charges on the walk in.
  • Reflective strip across the front for 6am car parks. Light grey outer, GTKF blue lining inside so black items are visible.
  • Wipe-clean inside. Sand gets in everything here; this one empties with a shake.

60-second pack check

Before every session

Five fingers, five things. Say them out loud as you touch each one in the bag. Tap the tiles as you go.

Bag's ready. Go move them.
“Whistle, wand, pen, pad, tassels. Go.”

The five tiles share their ticks with the ★ items in the kit list above, so a full pack check is also a packed bag.

End of session, same five in reverse. Tassels counted back in, pad in its sleeve, pen in the loop, wand in the spine, whistle off the neck and into the red pocket. Then zip.

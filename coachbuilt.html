Straight answers

The things you're already thinking.

I already pay for Clubworx / Mindbody / Glofox. Why would I change?

Because that software was built to fit ten thousand businesses, so it fits yours about 70%. The other 30% is the workaround you've got in a spreadsheet, the thing you explain to every new staff member, and the four taps it takes to do something that should take one.

And their price climbs as you grow — more members, more locations, more per month. Mine doesn't. You're also handing your members an app with someone else's brand on it, at the exact moment they're deciding whether they're part of something.

Do I own it, or am I renting from you now instead?

Fair question — it's the right one to ask. Your data is yours outright, from day one, exportable to a spreadsheet whenever you like, including the day you leave.

The system runs on my hosting while you're a client, which is what keeps it at one flat fee instead of a build invoice. After twelve months you can buy it out and take the code. And I'll put that in writing before you pay anything.

What if I need something changed?

You message me and I build it. No quote, no change request form, no "that's out of scope".

That's the actual point of paying monthly instead of once — a system that gets shaped around you for as long as you're running it, rather than one that was right in the week it launched and slowly stopped fitting.

Five days? That can't be a real system.

It's five days because I'm not starting from a blank page. Sign-ups, check-ins, bookings, staff logins with permissions, push notifications, admin desks — I've already built every one of those, twice over, in two apps that are live right now. Your build is those pieces arranged around how you run your place, not invented from scratch.

I use AI heavily for the mechanical work, which is why it's days instead of months. What isn't automated is knowing what a coach actually needs on a Tuesday night — and that part I've lived.

The honest limit: day five is your core system — branding, sign-ups, timetable, bookings, attendance and your admin screen. Taking money through it lands the week after, because that waits on your Stripe account and their verification, not on me. I'll say the same thing on the call, before you've paid anything.

What happens if you get sick of it, or hit by a bus?

Your data exports any time, so you're never locked in a box. And it's written into the agreement that if I stop operating, the code comes to you. You'd be moving on with everything in hand, which is more than your current software would give you.

I'm small — thirty members and a group chat. Am I wasting your time?

Possibly, and I'll tell you on the call rather than take your money. At thirty members a group chat and a spreadsheet genuinely might be right, and I'd rather you come back at a hundred and fifty than resent a bill at thirty.

If you're growing fast, though, the best time to put the system in is just before you need it — not in the middle of the mess.

Is this just a website?

No. A website is a poster. This is the thing your business runs on: your members sign up in it, book in it, get reminded by it, pay through it, and you open a desk that tells you who showed, who's drifting and what the month is worth.

A website can be part of it if you want one. It's the least interesting part.

Start here

The first seven days.

You don't need an audience, a logo or a business name registered to get the first client. You need twenty conversations. This is how you get them.

Day 1

Put the link up

Bio link → your page. Post reel 1 (the origin one). Don't explain it in the caption, let the reel do it.

Day 2

Write the list

Twenty gyms, clubs and academies you could name right now. See who to hit. Twenty. Not five.

Day 3

Send ten DMs

The cold one below. It's a question, not a pitch. Ten today, ten tomorrow. No follow-ups yet.

Day 4

Post reel 2, send ten more

Reel 2 is the "add it up" one — it's the best hook you've got. Expect the first real replies here.

Day 5

Book calls

Anyone who replied with a real answer, move to a call. "Got twenty minutes Tuesday?" Nothing fancier.

Day 6

Post reel 3, one follow-up round

One follow-up to the silent ones, then leave them alone forever. The follow-up script is below.

Day 7

Take the calls

Use the call script. Don't quote before you've played the problem back to them.

Then

Repeat until one says yes

Twenty DMs is usually three to five conversations and one or two calls. You need roughly three rounds of that to land the first client. That's the actual maths — keep going.

Content

Six reels. Post one every two days.

Same structure every time, because it works: say what they think out loud → show something specific → soft CTA. Shot on your phone, talking to camera, no editing tricks. The hook is the first three seconds — if that's flat, nothing else matters.

Two rules, non-negotiable

Targets

Where the twenty come from.

  • 1. Every basketball club and academy you've played against, trained at, or know a coach at. Start here — this is your actual edge and nobody else has it.
  • 2. The coaches in your phone. Not the gyms — the individual coaches who run their own programs. They're the easiest yes and they'll refer you upward.
  • 3. Instagram: search your suburb + "basketball academy", "strength gym", "PT studio", "junior program". Follow, watch a few reels, then DM. Don't DM cold off a search result with no context.
  • 4. Anyone who's ever posted "sorry for the late notice" or a payment reminder to their members' story. That's a person with the exact problem you solve, advertising it publicly.
  • 5. Gyms whose booking link is a Google Form or a DM. Instant qualifier — they've already outgrown what they've got.

Who to say no to

Under 50 members with no growth plan, anyone who wants to pay per feature, anyone who says "can you just build it cheap and I'll pay you when it makes money", and anyone who needs it integrated with a system they won't give you access to. Saying no to those keeps you alive for the good ones.

The call

Twenty minutes, in this order.

You're not presenting. You're diagnosing, then repeating their problem back to them more clearly than they said it. That's the moment they decide to buy — before you've named a price.

  1. 0–2 min · Open and shut up "Thanks for the time. Just walk me through how the place actually runs day to day." Then say nothing. Let them talk for two minutes without you filling the gaps.
  2. 2–10 min · The five questions Ask these, in this order, and write down their exact words:
    Ask exactly these
    1. How does someone new actually sign up right now — walk me through it like I'm them. 2. How do you take the roll, and where does it end up? 3. How do people pay you, and how do you know who hasn't? 4. What do you end up doing on a Sunday night that you shouldn't be doing? 5. If I could only fix one of those, which one?
  3. 10–14 min · Play it back "So the actual job here is [their thing, in their words]. Everything else is downstream of that." If they say "yeah, exactly" — you've sold it. If they correct you, even better, ask more.
  4. 14–18 min · What you'd build Describe their system in their language, not features. "You'd have one screen on a Sunday that tells you who trained and who hasn't paid, and the parents get told automatically." Then the five days, then the "if it's not right you walk" line.
  5. 18–20 min · Price, then silence Say the number. Stop talking. Do not fill the pause, do not justify it, do not offer a discount into the silence — that pause is them deciding, and whoever talks first loses.
  6. Close "I can start Monday. I'll send you a one-pager tonight with exactly what's in it and the six-month terms — have a read and tell me yes or no by Friday." Book the build date on the call.

The one rule

Never quote before you've played their problem back to them. A price with no diagnosis is just a number to compare against Mindbody. A price after the diagnosis is the cost of fixing the thing they just spent ten minutes complaining about.

Hold these lines

  • Never discount the monthly. Drop the scope instead. A discount tells them the first number was made up.
  • Never do a free build "for the portfolio". You already have a portfolio — it's your two apps.
  • Never price per member. That's the trap the incumbents are in and the reason you beat them.
  • First client can have a founding rate if you want one — but call it that, make it permanent for them, and never repeat it. "Founding rate, locked for as long as you stay" is a gift. "I'll do you a deal" is a leak.

After they say yes

What five days can and can't be.

Five days is a promise you can keep — but only if you're exact about what Friday means. The fastest way to lose the first client is to let them imagine more than you said.

Friday — promise these

  • Their branding: name, logo, colours, their own link
  • Member sign-up and a member list you can search
  • Timetable and bookings, with capacity
  • Attendance / check-in, however they actually take a roll
  • An admin screen: who's in, who's booked, who's gone quiet
  • Installs on a phone from a link

The week after — say this on the call, not later

Taking money through it. The payment link to their Stripe is the one piece that doesn't depend on how fast you build — it waits on them opening the account and Stripe verifying their business, which takes as long as it takes. Volunteering that is what makes the other five days believable. Hiding it is how a great first week turns into a bad second one.

Say "month two" to these — out loud, on the call

  • Migrating years of old member history out of their current system
  • Direct debit, refunds, failed-payment chasing, anything accounting-shaped
  • Integrating with software they can't give you a login for
  • An App Store / Play Store listing (it installs from a link — that's the point)
  • Push notifications if you're on Court One — that's a Full Court thing

Naming these before they ask makes you look like you've done it fifty times. Getting caught out by one in week three makes you look like you haven't.

Take these on day one

Their logo file, their colours, their current member list as a spreadsheet, their timetable, their Stripe login (they create it, you connect it), and one sentence describing every membership type they sell. If you don't have all seven, the five days hasn't started — say that plainly and put the Monday back. A clock you start without the inputs is a clock you miss.

Read this bit properly

  • Three clients is the real goal. Not ten. Three paying clients is about $39k a year and, more importantly, three case studies — which is what turns cold DMs into inbound.
  • Somewhere around eight, delivery becomes the constraint, not sales — and five-day builds don't change that, because the load isn't the build, it's the eight clients all wanting "one new feature a month" on top of support. Put the price up before you hit that, not after.
  • Cashflow is the catch of monthly-only. You carry a full build before a single dollar arrives, every time. At five days that's survivable — but it's one build week at a time. Two clients starting the same Monday isn't two weeks of work, it's one bad week and two disappointed people. Stagger the start dates.
  • The six-month minimum is what makes this survivable. $890 × 6 = $5,340 guaranteed per client. Don't ever let someone talk you out of it; it's the only thing standing in for the build fee you're not charging.
